A day in the house

  1. Feed, water, clean. Fill the bowls with seed, pellets, fruit or nuts, refill the water, scrub the perches. The three icons top-left track it.
  2. Then the birds themselves. Pet, whistle, play, hand over treats, comfort whoever is frightened, medicate whoever is unwell. Trust is earned daily.
  3. Then Adrian. Answer the phone, put a record on, sit in the garden, look away from what hurts to look at.
  4. Sleep. The day ends when he lets it, and the house is different in the morning.

What this is

Adrian Hale has stopped leaving the hill. Across the garden, under fourteen metres of glass, six parrots depend on him entirely, and that is the only appointment he still keeps.

Sanity here is not a bar. It changes what you see, what you hear, what the records say, and whether the hallway ends where the hallway ended yesterday. The birds remember how they were treated. Some of them learn things nobody remembers saying.

Every sprite, room and bird is drawn procedurally at runtime and every sound is synthesized in the browser — no image or audio files.
Progress saves in this browser when Adrian sleeps.
